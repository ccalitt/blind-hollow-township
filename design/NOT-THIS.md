# Not-This List — Hollow Township

> Status: FROZEN. Explicit exclusions to prevent scope creep and design drift.
> If a feature appears on this list, it is cut without discussion.

---

## Not a Horror Game That Happens to Have a Map

The simulation layer is load-bearing. It is not set dressing for the horror. Township management, faction politics, and resource decisions are the primary gameplay surface. Horror is the consequence layer, not the content layer.

## Not a City Builder With Horror Flavor

The simulation exists to create moral weight, not to be satisfying in isolation. If the building system is fun without the horror consequences, the design has drifted. Players should not be able to separate "the fun part" from "the scary part."

## Not a Binary Moral Choice System

No good/evil sliders. No karma meters. No clearly labeled "be a hero" vs. "be a monster" forks. Moral weight must be embedded in actions that feel mechanical until consequence surfaces. See `PILLARS.md` Pillar 3.

## Not a Jump Scare Game

Atmospheric audio stings and visual startles may exist but are not the horror. The horror is the realization that the thing threatening you tonight is the thing you built yesterday. No horror sequence should work on a player who has not made any choices yet.

## Not an Open World

Chapter-based structure only. Each chapter is a contained moral argument with a beginning, middle, and end. Open world would diffuse the moral accumulation that makes Macro choices land.

## Not a Roguelike

Player choices persist. The world has memory. Death (if it exists) must carry narrative consequence, not a clean restart. Procedural generation is used for variety within a designed structure, not as a replacement for authored stakes.

## Not a Punishing Survival Game

Difficulty is not the point. The player should not die because they lacked skill. Consequence is the point. A player who plays "well" by survival game standards must still face moral cost.

## Not a Comedy or Satire

Tone is horror drama. Irony and dark humor may exist in NPC dialogue — they are pressure valves, not genre signals. The game does not wink at the player about its own darkness.

## Not Multiplayer (at Launch)

Single-player only at launch. Multiplayer would compromise the "Watched Feeling" — the system adapts to *you*, not a consensus. Social play dilutes personal moral weight.

## Not a Free Demo

Chapter 1 is paid Early Access content. Framing as a demo or prologue undervalues the content and targets the wrong audience metric (downloads vs. wishlist-to-purchase conversion).

## Features That Sound Right But Aren't

| Sounds Like It Fits | Why It Doesn't |
|---------------------|----------------|
| Dialogue wheel with obvious options | Makes moral weight legible — removes earned dread |
| Survival meters (hunger, sleep, sanity) | Redirects moral weight to resource management, not people |
| Upgrade trees | Creates optimal play path, removes moral uncertainty |
| Day recap / summary screen | Tells the player what their choices meant — they should figure that out |
| Random events at night | Breaks the "night traces to day" rule — see Pillar 4 |
| Safe houses / zones with no corruption risk | Gives player a retreat that removes dread |
| Achievement system for moral choices | Makes ethics into a collection game |
