# Design Pillars — Hollow Township

> Status: FROZEN. These are design gates. A feature that kills a pillar is cut, not adjusted.

---

## Pillar 1 — Earned Dread

**Statement**: Horror must be earned through player action, not scripted atmosphere.

The dread the player feels must be directly traceable to choices they made. Random horror is decoration. Consequential horror is the game.

**Enables**:
- Night events shaped by Day choices
- Horror intensity scaling with accumulated moral debt
- Players recognizing their own decisions as the source of what hunts them

**Kills**:
- Jump scares disconnected from player state
- Horror that cannot be traced back to a choice
- Scripted horror sequences that play identically for all players

---

## Pillar 2 — The Watched Feeling

**Statement**: The player must feel the world is observing and responding to them specifically.

Not a generic adaptive difficulty system. The game adapts so every positive outcome carries a visible cost, and every cost carries a visible trade. The player cannot optimize their way out of moral weight.

**Enables**:
- Named NPCs whose behavior changes based on player choices
- Faction systems with memory (they remember what you did on Day 1)
- Asymmetric outcomes — a choice that was right in one context becomes wrong in another

**Kills**:
- Stat-only consequences with no narrative surface
- NPCs without names, without memory, without arcs
- Any system where optimal play removes moral weight

---

## Pillar 3 — Moral Weight at Every Turn

**Statement**: Every meaningful action is a moral choice, even when it feels like logistics.

The player must not always know they are making a moral choice. Building placement, NPC job assignment, and resource allocation are moral choices — they feel mechanical until night reveals what they were.

Three tiers of moral weight:
- **Micro** (every 10–15 min): Invisible. Feels like gameplay. Cost surfaces in next night or next day cycle.
- **Mid** (every 30–45 min): Slightly uncomfortable. Cost surfaces within same day or same night.
- **Macro** (once per chapter beat): Explicitly moral. Immediate impact + second-order delayed effect.

**Enables**:
- Logistics UI that is secretly a moral interface
- Late reveals that recontextualize earlier "neutral" choices
- Compulsion loop activation from minute 20 (not hour 3)

**Kills**:
- Clearly labeled "good/evil" binary choices
- Moral choices that cost nothing mechanically
- Choice systems that allow replaying for the optimal branch

---

## Pillar 4 — Day/Night as Structural Argument

**Statement**: The day/night cycle is not a feature — it is the game's argument about human nature.

Day = what we build (Relief). Night = what that building costs (Unease). Dawn = the compulsion to continue despite knowing the cost (Compulsion).

**Critical rule**: Night outcomes must be shaped by Day choices. If night horror is random, the "watched" feeling breaks. Players must trace night events back to day decisions.

**Enables**:
- Day phase (building/politics/drama) as the Relief beat of the engagement loop
- Night phase (horror/dread/corruption) as the Unease beat
- Dawn as the Compulsion beat — the pull to go back and do it again
- Three-beat engagement loop: RELIEF → UNEASE → COMPULSION

**Kills**:
- Nighttime horror independent of daytime choices
- Day phase that is pure positive-feedback city building with no dread foreshadowing
- Night phase that can be "solved" with enough stat investment

---

## Pillar 5 — Genre Whitespace

**Statement**: No existing game occupies the intersection of Strong Horror + Strong Simulation + Strong Narrative Drama simultaneously. This is the target position.

Comparable games dominate only one or two axes:
- Frostpunk: Strong Simulation + Moderate Drama, minimal horror
- This War of Mine: Strong Drama + Moderate Horror, no simulation depth
- Disco Elysium: Strong Drama + Moderate Horror, no simulation
- Darkest Dungeon: Strong Horror + Moderate Simulation, thin narrative drama

**Enables**:
- Clear market positioning with no direct competitor
- Press/streamer angle: "finally"
- Mechanics from all three genres reinforcing each other

**Kills**:
- Sacrificing horror depth to make the simulation friendly
- Sacrificing simulation depth to make the drama faster
- Sacrificing drama stakes to keep the horror clean
