# Interaction Model — Hollow Township

> Status: FROZEN (2026-04-26)
> Defines what the player does with mouse and keyboard. The player is the township manager — a disembodied presence. The player character is the camera and the cursor.
> Pillar 3: Logistics choices are moral choices. The interaction model must make them feel entirely like logistics.

---

## 1. Primary Input Model

**Device**: Mouse + keyboard. Controller support: Phase 2.

**Two cursor modes. No others.**

**Observe mode** (default): Neutral crosshair. Hover over any world object shows its name only — no stats, no moral labels. Click selects it and opens its context panel on the right side of screen.

**Place mode**: Activated when a building type is selected from the build menu. Cursor becomes a placement ghost — the building footprint follows the cursor, snapping to grid. Click places. Right-click or Escape cancels and returns to Observe mode.

No drag-select. No multi-unit commands. One object, one decision, one consequence.

**Keyboard**: Camera pan (WASD or arrow keys), zoom (scroll wheel or +/-), rotate Y-axis (Q/E or middle-mouse drag). Build menu toggle (B or dedicated hotkey). Pause (Space or P). All interaction with world objects is mouse-only.

---

## 2. Building Placement

**Build menu**: Single button, bottom of screen (keyboard shortcut: B). Presents available building types as an icon-and-name list. No stats listed. No "effects" listed. The player does not know what a building does to the world state until it does it.

**Placement**: Grid-snap. Buildings snap to valid cells. Invalid cells — hollow-adjacent land, occupied footprints — cause the placement ghost to turn red. No tooltip. No explanation. Red means no.

**Confirmation**: None. The choice is made when the building lands.

**Visual feedback**: Building fades in over 1 second. No notification. No counter increment. The building is there. That is the feedback.

## 3. NPC Interaction and Assignment

**To assign an NPC**: Click on the NPC in the world. Context panel opens on the right side of screen.

**Context panel contents**:
- NPC name
- Current assignment
- Available assignments as a plain text list — e.g., "Mill Quarter, morning shift" / "Eastern Ridge, night watch" / "Incomplete Structure, day labor"
- One line of current behavioral state in the NPC's voice register (not a stat — a sentence reflecting their actual state, generated by the NPC Behavior Agent)
- For new arrivals only: one skill signal line drawn from their former-life background (see NPC_INFLOW_PRODUCTIVITY.md — this is the player's only signal for skill domain and depth)

**Behavioral state examples** (not templates — register illustrations):
- Elias: "Hasn't slept. Asks about the treeline survey."
- Peder: "Wants to know if the foundation scope has changed."
- Maren: "Has been at the ledger since before the mill started."

**Arrival skill signal examples** (not templates — these replace the behavioral state line on Day 1 of arrival):
- Construction/Practiced: "Twelve years framing residential builds. Knows which joints hold in cold weather."
- Medicine/Surface: "Worked construction most of her life." *(incorrect domain: this is what it looks like when signal and domain don't obviously match)*
- Logistics/Deep: "Structural engineer. She can read a foundation the way Thomas reads a patient."

The skill signal line is the player's only information about the arrival's former-life domain. It is not labeled as a skill. There is no "best match" indicator. The player reads it and infers. The panel closes on assignment. The inference is not confirmed.

This line changes as arcs change. It is the only NPC voice accessible during day phase outside of scripted mid/macro choice moments. It is not a mood meter.

**Assignment**: Click the task name. Panel closes. NPC walks to their post. Done.

**Bond acknowledgment (Day 3+)**: When two NPCs have been co-assigned to the same zone for 2 consecutive days, clicking either NPC surfaces one additional text option in their context panel:
- "Keep them working together."
This is a logged choice. It creates a bond entry in `player_choices` with both `npc_ids`. It does not prevent the player from separating them — they can still reassign either NPC. The option does not appear on Day 1 or Day 2 — only after the bond condition has been met. The game does not explain what the option means. Selecting it or ignoring it are both valid. The dawn consequence thread has a legal Sentence 1 ("You kept Elias and the arrival working the eastern ridge through Day 3.") when bond vulnerability later surfaces.

**For new arrivals — first conversation**: After role assignment, the panel offers one additional option: initiate a first conversation. This is not a dialogue tree. It is a single text option:
- "Ask how they're doing." (initiates; has arc and settling-path consequences)
The option does not appear on Day 2. If the player does not select it on Day 1 of arrival, the conversation does not happen. The game does not note the omission.

No dialogue trees. No confirmation. No "are you sure you want to assign Elias to Eastern Ridge?"

## 4. Faction Interaction

**Faction representatives appear as named NPCs in the Settlement Common** during their active moments: Day 1 morning (introductions) and Hour 1:45 (mid-weight choice).

**Click on faction representative**: Context panel opens. Text list of interaction options. Same visual treatment as NPC assignment — plain text, no moral framing.

**Hour 1:45 example — The Harrow's Crossing Compact**:
- "Sign the ratification document."
- "Request time to review."
- "Ask about the survey's history."

**Hour 1:45 example — Field Agent Dix**:
- "Approve the site inspection."
- "Delay the inspection. Safety conditions."
- "Deny the request."

Options are described by what they are. No label for weight, risk, or moral category. The weight is in the content, not the label.

**After selection**: Panel closes. The faction representative walks away or delivers a single in-world response line — no modal popup, no confirmation. The second-order consequence is scheduled in the backend. Faction negotiations in Chapter 1 are one-round. The player states their position once. The faction responds in behavior, not in further dialogue.

---

## 5. Night Phase Input

**Player can**: Pan camera, zoom, observe.

**Player cannot**: Issue orders, reassign NPCs, place buildings, interact with faction representatives.

**Clicking an NPC at night**: Context panel shows NPC name and current state only. No assignment options. Elias is on the Eastern Ridge because that is where he was placed. The player can see him. The player cannot move him.

**Pause**: Permitted. World freezes — visually intact, silent, and present. The night log does not advance. Pending events do not arrive. The player sees exactly what they were looking at, stopped. The game does not make pausing feel like safety.

**The game does not tell the player their orders are locked.** They discover it when they click on Maren and there is nothing to assign. The absence of an option is the information.

---

## 6. What the UI Never Does

- Confirms or warns before any day-phase choice
- Labels an option as moral, dangerous, costly, or good
- Shows secondary effects, downstream consequences, or probability of outcomes
- Summarizes what the player chose or why it mattered
- Addresses the player by role ("Manager" is not used)
- Explains why a placement cell is invalid
- Tells the player their night-phase orders are locked before they try to issue one
